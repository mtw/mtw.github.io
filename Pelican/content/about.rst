    Michael T. Wolfinger is a computational RNA biologist working on RNA structure, folding kinetics, synthetic RNA design, RNA-protein recognition, and structured viral RNAs. His research combines thermodynamic modeling, comparative analysis, molecular simulation, and machine learning, with the aim of making computational results mechanistically useful rather than merely descriptive.

    He leads research as a Principal Investigator at the University of Vienna, Austria, and is affiliated with the :link-flat:`Theoretical Biochemistry Group (TBI) <http://www.tbi.univie.ac.at/>`, a long-standing center of RNA bioinformatics and home of the :link-flat:`ViennaRNA Package <http://www.tbi.univie.ac.at/RNA>`. In 2023 and 2024, he served as a visiting professor at the University of Freiburg.

    The broader thread running through his work is the question of how much biological and engineering insight can be extracted once RNA structure is treated as a primary object. That question connects projects in RNA secondary and tertiary structure prediction, kinetics-aware RNA design, comparative RNA virology, and structure-guided analysis of RNA-protein interactions.

    In addition to academic research, he founded :link-flat:`RNA Forecast <https://rnaforecast.com>`, a venture focused on the practical use of RNA structure knowledge in biotechnology and drug development. That work includes workshops, design reviews, and focused advisory support for research groups and biotech teams that need external expertise in computational RNA biology.

    For a structured overview of the main scientific themes, see the :link-flat:`research page <{filename}/research.rst>`. For the formal publication record, see :link-flat:`publications <{filename}/publications.rst>`. Many of the underlying papers are discussed in more detail on the :link-flat:`blog </blog/>`.

Academic Profile
================

Michael T. Wolfinger works at the interface of structural biology, synthetic biology, and machine learning, with a consistent emphasis on RNA. His publication record spans method development, comparative genomics, virus bioinformatics, RNA design, and molecular modeling. Across those areas, the recurring goal is to develop models that remain interpretable and useful in real biological settings.

This includes work on:

.. raw:: html

   <ul>
     <li>RNA structure prediction informed by thermodynamics, probing data, and comparative evidence</li>
     <li>folding kinetics and cotranscriptional effects in regulatory RNAs and designed RNA systems</li>
     <li>comparative analysis of structured viral RNAs, especially in flaviviral untranslated regions</li>
     <li>RNA-protein interaction modeling and structure-guided refinement workflows</li>
     <li>machine learning methods where they accelerate a well-defined RNA analysis problem</li>
   </ul>


Teaching and Collaboration
==========================

Teaching and collaborative work are a substantial part of this profile. Over the years, Michael T. Wolfinger has taught computational RNA biology in academic settings and worked with collaborators across virology, structural biology, synthetic biology, and RNA bioinformatics. That breadth is reflected both in the publication record and in the workshop formats described on the :link-flat:`services page </services>`.
